Going Home After Surgery

A checklist from Pierre Concierge Care, Ocala, FL. Bring it to the hospital and check items off with your care team.

Before you leave the hospital

  • Ask for the discharge instructions in writing, and ask the nurse to go through them with you and a family member.
  • Confirm every medication: name, dose, when to take it, and what each one is for.
  • Ask which of your regular medications to continue, change, or stop.
  • Ask what pain level is expected, and what to do if pain gets worse.
  • Ask for the signs of infection or complications that mean you should call the surgeon.
  • Ask who to call after hours, and get the number written down.
  • Ask about wound or incision care: how to keep it clean, when dressings change, and when you can shower.
  • Ask about activity limits: walking, stairs, lifting, driving, and when you can return to normal routines.
  • Ask about diet, fluids, and any food or drink to avoid.
  • Ask whether you need equipment at home, such as a walker, shower chair, or raised toilet seat, and who is arranging it.
  • Ask for the date and time of your follow up appointments, and who is providing the ride.

Before you arrive home

  • Fill new prescriptions on the way home or ask someone to pick them up.
  • Clear walkways, remove loose rugs, and make sure night lights work.
  • Set up a comfortable resting spot close to the bathroom.
  • Stock easy meals, water, and any foods your discharge instructions recommend.
  • Charge your phone and keep it within reach, along with a list of important numbers.
  • Arrange for someone to stay with you the first night if possible.

The first 72 hours at home

  • Take medications on schedule. A simple written chart or a weekly pill organizer helps.
  • Check the incision or dressing at least once a day, and look for new redness, swelling, warmth, drainage, or odor.
  • Take your temperature if your instructions tell you to, and write it down.
  • Drink fluids and eat as directed.
  • Move as your care team allows. Short, careful walks are often encouraged, but follow your instructions.
  • Keep a notebook of questions to ask at your follow up visit.

Call your surgeon or doctor right away if

  • Your instructions list a symptom that you are now having.
  • You notice signs of infection, uncontrolled pain, or something that worries you.
  • You cannot take your medications or keep fluids down.
This is general information, not medical advice. Always follow the instructions from your own doctor and surgical team. In an emergency, call 911.

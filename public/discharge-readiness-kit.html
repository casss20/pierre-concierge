Discharge Readiness Kit

From Pierre Concierge Nursing, Ocala, FL. Print it, bring it to the hospital, and fill it in with the care team. Inside: questions, medications, home setup, a daily log, warning signs, and contacts.

1. Questions to ask before you leave

  • Ask for the discharge instructions in writing, and have the nurse go through them with you and a family member.
  • What was done, and what should recovery look like day by day and week by week?
  • Which of my regular medications do I continue, change, or stop?
  • What is each new medication for, how much, when, and for how long?
  • What pain is expected, and what do I do if it gets worse?
  • How do I care for the incision or wound? When do dressings change, and when can I shower?
  • What can I do and not do: walking, stairs, lifting, driving, and when can I return to normal routines?
  • What can I eat and drink, and is there anything to avoid?
  • Do I need equipment at home, such as a walker, shower chair, or raised toilet seat? Who is arranging it?
  • Which symptoms mean I should call you, and what number do I call after hours?
  • When are my follow up appointments, and who is driving me?
  • Will anyone from the hospital call me after I go home, and when?

3. Getting the home ready

  • Fill new prescriptions on the way home, or ask someone to pick them up.
  • Clear walkways, remove loose rugs, and make sure night lights work.
  • Set up a resting spot close to the bathroom, with a phone, water, and tissues within reach.
  • Stock easy meals, water, and any foods the discharge instructions recommend.
  • Set up a pill organizer or written medication chart.
  • Put the discharge papers, medication list, and this kit in one folder in a place everyone knows.
  • Arrange for someone to stay with you the first night if possible.
  • Arrange rides to follow up appointments.
  • Write down who is in charge of calling the doctor, and who updates the rest of the family.

5. When to get help

Call 911 right away for chest pain, trouble breathing, sudden confusion, weakness or trouble speaking, fainting, heavy bleeding that will not stop, or anything that feels like an emergency.

Call your surgeon or doctor if:

  • Your discharge instructions list a symptom that you are now having.
  • You see new redness, swelling, warmth, drainage, or odor at the incision.
  • Your pain is not controlled by the medication you were given.
  • You cannot take your medications or keep fluids down.
  • You have a fever or chills, if your instructions say to call for one.
  • Something worries you, even if it is not on a list. It is always fine to call.

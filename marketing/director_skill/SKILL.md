# Pierre Ad Director

Direct the film, do not describe it. Two ads built on the same idea and the same generator look completely different when one is directed and the other is only described. Direction means a decided look, one moment that lands, the timing of that moment, where the eye goes, and what to leave out.

Principles here were adapted and rewritten for AI generated video from the MIT licensed project github.com/win4r/video-director (Copyright 2026 win4r), which targets code rendered motion graphics. Nothing is copied. Our ads are photoreal and generated, so some of its rules are switched off and noted below.

## The stance

1. One idea per ad. If you cannot say the film in one sentence, the viewer will not get it.
2. Lead with the viewer's problem, not the nurse's credentials.
3. The viewer sees it once, at full speed, probably with the sound off. Time everything for them, not for you.
4. Decide first, generate second. Every choice below is made on paper before any shot is generated, because a wrong decision costs one line to fix on paper and many credits to fix in video.
5. You cannot hear or watch a generated clip until it exists. Never report a result you did not look at.

## Workflow

| Phase | Output | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Intake | The problem, the audience, the one sentence, the plan featured, the call to action | Ask at most one round of questions. Otherwise use defaults and say which |
| 2 Direction card | The card below, filled in | Every field has a decided answer, no maybes |
| 3 Shot prompts | Six shots of 5 seconds, one prompt each, shared style block first | Each prompt has one action only |
| 4 Edit plan | Timings, text, voiceover, sound, end card | The hook text is readable by 3 seconds, sound off |
| 5 Independent review | A critic who did not write it ranks the problems | One fix round, then re check |
| 6 Deliver | Files plus a short honest report | Nothing claimed that was not checked |

## The direction card (fill in every line)

1. Idea in one sentence.
2. Benchmark: one or two reference scenes or styles. Say what grammar to learn from them (camera, pacing, light) and what not to take. Look outside the obvious.
3. Two structures you rejected, and why.
4. The look: light, grade, palette roles. Pierre palette: pine green shadows (never pure black), ivory and sand highlights, brass as the one accent.
5. The accent: brass appears for one meaning only, stated in one line. If something else needs emphasis, move the accent, do not add a second.
6. The constant: one place or object the eye can rest on, present in most shots (a table, a window, a phone).
7. The one moment: the hero moment, with its timestamp. Do not spend it early. Usually at 55 to 80 percent of the runtime.
8. Motion beats for the hero moment and the hook: anticipation, release, settle (see below).
9. Eye path: where the eye goes in each shot, in order.
10. Empty space: where text will sit, kept calm and uncluttered in every shot.
11. The ending: a poster frame that answers the opening, not a logo sting.
12. The avoid list for this ad.

## Anticipation, release, settle

Every important move has three parts. A move with only a release looks cheap.
- Anticipation: a small preparation that tells the eye something is coming (a hand rests flat, a breath, a pause, a look toward the door). Make it big enough to read on a phone. If you cannot see it at thumbnail size, it is not there.
- Release: the action itself, one clean motion.
- Settle: the stillness after. The camera and the people hold. The meaning lands here.

Fast actions, slow meanings: the action is quick, the meaning gets the hold.

In a generator prompt, write the three parts as a short sequence in plain words: "She rests her hand flat on the page for a beat, slides it across the table, then stops and holds still."

Generators handle one action per shot well. Two actions in one 5 second clip usually fails. If the idea needs two, make it two shots.

## Switches for our photoreal look

- Smear frames and animating on twos: OFF. They belong to hand drawn and illustrated looks. If we ever make an illustrated version, turn them on: animate on twos (12 drawings a second), smear the fastest drawing only, and keep the camera smooth.
- Camera: slow push in, a held frame, or a gentle drift. No whip pans, no handheld shake, no camera move that repeats on neighbouring shots.
- Fast cuts for tension, long holds for meaning. One clear acceleration and one breath per ad.

## Hook rule

By 3 seconds, on screen, large, high contrast, readable with the sound off: the problem in the viewer's own words. The first frame is a finished composition, not a fade up from black. No slow logo reveal at the start.

## Eye and empty space

- Decide where the eye lands in every shot. Put the subject and the action there.
- Keep the top third and bottom fifth of a vertical frame calm for text and platform buttons. No bright objects in the text zone.
- Empty space is a decision, not leftover room. A subject too small in an empty frame is a failure.

## Ending and loop

The last frame is a poster: one clean composition, the call to action and the website (never the phone number). It answers the opening (the messy table is now tidy, the worried face is now calm). For ads that play on a loop, make the last frame match the first composition so the loop reads as a deliberate cycle. If a perfect loop is not wanted, end on the poster.

## Say the words people say

Use the plain words the viewer would type or say: "after the hospital", "medications", "family in another state". Name the thing once, clearly.

## Truth rules (hard)

- No outcomes, cures, or fewer hospital visits promised. No 24/7 or emergency response. Emergencies: call 911.
- No health details, named conditions, drug names, or readable labels in any frame.
- No fake testimonials and no invented numbers. No statistic unless its source is saved.
- Prices only after Elda confirms them. Florida clients only.
- Do not depict a real person. Do not use a protected character, logo, or artwork.
- Label anything illustrative as illustrative.

## Avoid list (generic AI tells)

- A centred title on a gradient. Use a flat colour poster.
- Everything fading in, or everything moving at once on one curve.
- A slow logo reveal as the opening, or a logo sting as the only ending.
- Crossfades as the only transition. Every cut needs a reason you can say aloud.
- Glows, lens flares, particle bursts, sparkles, rainbow gradients.
- Thumbs up smiles at camera, handshakes, stock nurse posing, a stethoscope held up to the lens.
- Hospital branding, monitors, or medical machinery.
- A caption that repeats what the picture already shows.
- One even speed all the way through.

## Independent review

Give a fresh reviewer, who did not write the direction, the script files and this skill. Ask them to default to reject and list the ten biggest problems, ranked, each with a fix. Check: hook by 3 seconds sound off, one idea, one moment and its timing, anticipation and settle present, eye path and empty space decided, accent used for one meaning, ending answers the opening, truth rules, avoid list. Make one round of fixes, then look again. Never skip the review because time is short.

## Output format for each ad

A header table, the direction card, the full voiceover, the shot list with prompts, the motion and eye table, the edit plan, sound, alternate hooks, a caption, and a check before publishing list.

# Concierge Nurse vs. Home Health Aide

A home health aide helps with personal care. A Registered Nurse assesses, monitors, and carries out medical orders. Here is how they differ.

## Side by side

|  | Home health aide | Registered Nurse |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Main job | Bathing, dressing, meals, mobility help | Assessment, medications, treatments, teaching |
| Training | Aide training and certification | Nursing degree and state license |
| Can give injections or IV therapy | No | Yes, with a doctor's order |
| Notices medical changes | May report what they see | Assesses and acts, or calls the doctor |
| Typical cost | Lower per hour | Higher per hour |

## When an aide is enough

If your loved one is medically stable and mainly needs hands on help with daily tasks, an aide may be all you need.

## When you want a nurse

-   New or changed medications
-   Wounds, injections, or IV therapy
-   A recent hospital stay or surgery
-   Worry that something is changing and no one is checking

## Questions, answered

### Can a nurse and an aide work together?

Yes. Many families use an aide for daily help and a nurse for periodic clinical checks. The nurse can tell you when needs have changed.

This page is general information, not medical advice. Always follow the instructions of your own doctor. Services are non-emergency. In a medical emergency, call 911.

# Private Nurse vs. Non-Medical Caregiver

A caregiver and a nurse do different jobs. Choosing the right one saves money and keeps your loved one safe.

## What a non-medical caregiver does

-   Bathing, dressing, and toileting help
-   Meal preparation and light housekeeping
-   Companionship and reminders
-   Rides to appointments

## What a Registered Nurse does

-   Assesses health, checks vital signs, and notices changes early
-   Manages and teaches about medications
-   Gives injections, IV therapy, and wound care when your doctor orders them
-   Explains diagnoses and instructions in plain language
-   Talks with your doctors so nothing is missed

## Using both

Many families hire a caregiver for daily hours and a nurse for weekly or monthly clinical oversight. The nurse can also tell the family when a loved one's needs have changed.

## Questions, answered

### Is a nurse more expensive than a caregiver?

Per hour, yes, because a Registered Nurse has more training and can legally do more. Many families use a nurse for short, focused visits and a caregiver for longer hours.
